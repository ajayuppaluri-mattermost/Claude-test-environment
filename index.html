Day 1: Getting Started & Your First Tasks

Understand what Cowork is, set it up, and complete your first real task.

⏱ ~60–75 minutes

📖 Lesson 1.1 — What Is Claude Cowork?

Think of Claude Cowork as a capable assistant sitting at your computer. Unlike regular Claude chat (where you go back and forth in conversation), Cowork can actually do work on your behalf — create files, organize folders, build documents, and pull together research.

Chat vs. Cowork

💬 CHAT You ask, Claude answers. Like texting a smart friend. Claude describes how to do things.
⚡ COWORK You describe what you need done, Claude plans and executes it. Claude actually does the work and delivers finished files.

What Cowork Is Great At

  • Organizing messy folders — sorting, renaming, categorizing files
  • Creating polished documents, slide decks, and spreadsheets
  • Summarizing and analyzing information across multiple files
  • Research and report writing
  • Repetitive tasks you do regularly (daily briefings, weekly reports)

What Cowork Is NOT

  • It's not a coding tool (that's Claude Code)
  • It's still in "research preview" — very capable but still being improved
  • It only works while the Claude Desktop app is open on your computer

⚙️ Lesson 1.2 — Setting Up Cowork

1
Open the Claude Desktop App. Launch it on your computer. You'll see the familiar chat interface.
2
Find the Cowork Tab. Look at the top of the app or in the left sidebar. You'll see tabs including "Chat" and "Cowork" (or "Tasks"). Click Cowork.
3
Give Claude Access to a Folder. When you start a task, Claude will ask you to select a folder. Choose your "Cowork Practice" folder to experiment safely.
4
Understand the Safety Features (see below).

Safety Features You Should Know

  • Claude always asks permission before deleting files. You'll see a prompt and must click "Allow."
  • Claude only accesses folders you specifically grant. It cannot see your entire computer.
  • You can watch everything Claude does in real time. Progress indicators show each step.
  • You can jump in and redirect Claude at any time. You're always in control.
  • Your conversation history stays on your computer — not on Anthropic's servers.

🚀 Lesson 1.3 — Your First Cowork Task

Let's start with something simple and satisfying: organizing a folder.

Exercise: Organize a Folder

  1. Switch to Cowork mode in Claude Desktop.
  2. Select your "Cowork Practice" folder.
  3. Type the prompt below.
  4. Watch what happens: Claude will scan your files, create a plan, and present it.
  5. Approve or adjust: If the plan looks good, tell Claude to proceed. If not, just say what you'd change.
Help me organize this folder. Scan the contents and propose a plan: what categories/folders to create, how files should be sorted, and any naming conventions to apply. Show me the plan before making changes. Only proceed after I approve.

What you just learned: The "describe → review → approve" pattern. This is the foundation of everything in Cowork. You'll use this pattern constantly.

✍️ Lesson 1.4 — Writing Better Prompts

The quality of Cowork's output depends heavily on how you describe what you need.

Be Specific About the Outcome

❌ VAGUE "Make me a document about our team meeting."
✅ SPECIFIC "Create a Word document summarizing our team meeting. Include: date, attendees, key decisions, and action items with owners and deadlines."

Tell Claude What Format You Want

  • "Create a Word document (.docx)..."
  • "Build a PowerPoint presentation with 10 slides..."
  • "Make an Excel spreadsheet with formulas that calculate..."
  • "Generate a PDF report..."

Ask Claude to Show Its Plan First

For important tasks, always include: "Show me your approach before you start" or "Present a plan before executing."

Give Context About Your Audience

  • "This is for my CEO — keep it concise and executive-level."
  • "This will be shared with a non-technical team — avoid jargon."
  • "This is an internal draft — focus on accuracy over polish."

📄 Lesson 1.5 — Create a Document

Exercise: Create a Meeting Summary

  1. In Cowork mode, type the prompt below.
  2. Let Claude work. Watch the progress indicators.
  3. When finished, open the document from your folder and review it.
Create a Word document called "Weekly Team Update." Include a header with today's date, a section for "Key Accomplishments" with 3 bullet points (make up plausible examples), a section for "Upcoming Priorities" with 3 items, and a section for "Blockers or Risks" with 2 items. Use professional formatting with a clean, simple design. Save it to my folder.

🎉 Congratulations! You've completed Day 1. You can now set up Cowork, give it tasks, review its plans, and create professional documents.

📌 Day 1 Key Takeaways

  • Cowork = Claude that does work, not just answers questions
  • Always grant access to a specific folder — Claude can only see what you allow
  • Use the pattern: describe → review → approve (or redirect)
  • Be specific in your prompts: describe the outcome, format, and audience
  • Claude will always ask before deleting anything

Day 2: Real-World Tasks & Working With Your Files

Use Cowork for practical daily work — analyzing files, creating presentations, and multi-step projects.

⏱ ~60–90 minutes

📁 Lesson 2.1 — Working With Your Existing Files

Cowork really shines when it works with files you already have. Here are three patterns:

Analyzing a Document

Read the document [filename] in this folder. Give me a summary of the key points in plain language. Then create a new Word document called "Summary of [filename]" with: a 3-sentence executive summary at the top, followed by the main points organized by topic, and any action items or recommendations at the bottom.

Working With Spreadsheets

Open the spreadsheet [filename]. Tell me what data is in it — what columns are there, how many rows, and what the data appears to be about. Then create a clean summary of the key metrics or totals.

Working With Multiple Files

Look at all the documents in this folder. Create a single summary document that pulls out the key themes, decisions, and action items across all of them. Organize it chronologically.

Exercise: Pick a real document or spreadsheet from your work. Put it in your Cowork folder and ask Claude to summarize, analyze, or reorganize the information.

📊 Lesson 2.2 — Creating Presentations

One of Cowork's most popular uses. Here's the recipe for a great presentation prompt:

Include These Five Elements

  1. Topic and purpose — What is this about and why?
  2. Audience — Who will see this?
  3. Number of slides — Give a target (e.g., "8–10 slides")
  4. Structure — What should the flow look like?
  5. Style preferences — Professional? Minimal? Data-heavy?
Create a PowerPoint presentation called "Q1 Team Review." It should have 8 slides:

1. Title slide with the presentation name and today's date
2. Agenda slide listing what we'll cover
3. Key Metrics — use placeholder numbers for revenue, customer count, and satisfaction score
4. Wins — 3 major accomplishments this quarter
5. Challenges — 2 areas where we struggled
6. Lessons Learned — what we'd do differently
7. Next Quarter Priorities — top 3 focus areas
8. Q&A / Discussion closing slide

Keep the design clean and professional. Use a blue and white color scheme. Save it to my folder.

Exercise: Use the prompt above (or customize it to your own topic) and let Cowork build you a deck. Open it when done and review.

If you want changes, don't start over. Just tell Claude: "On slide 4, add a fourth bullet about customer retention" or "Change the color scheme to green and gray."

🔗 Lesson 2.3 — Multi-Step Projects

This is where Cowork gets really powerful. Describe a complex outcome and Claude breaks it down automatically.

How It Works

1
Plans — Breaks the work into subtasks
2
Coordinates — May run multiple subtasks in parallel using "sub-agents"
3
Executes — Works through each step, creating files and outputs
4
Delivers — Saves finished work to your folder
I need a briefing document on [topic of your choice]. Here's what I need:
1. Research the topic using web search
2. Create a Word document with an executive summary, 3–4 key trends with explanations, and a "What This Means for Us" section
3. Keep it under 3 pages
4. Use professional formatting with headers

Show me your research plan first before writing the report.

Exercise — choose one:

Option A (Travel Planning): "Research the top 5 family-friendly restaurants in [your city]. Create a Word document with the name, address, price range, what they're known for, and a recommendation for which to try first."

Option B (Competitive Research): "Research [a company you're interested in]. Create a one-page summary with: what they do, who their customers are, their recent news, and their main competitors."

🎛️ Lesson 2.4 — Steering and Correcting Mid-Task

You don't have to wait until Claude is finished. At any point during a task, you can:

  • Redirect: "Actually, focus more on the budget section and less on the timeline."
  • Add information: "By the way, our budget is $50,000 — include that number."
  • Pause and review: "Stop here and show me what you have so far before continuing."
  • Change course: "This isn't quite right. Instead of a report, make it a bulleted email I can send to my team."

Claude will adjust without losing the work it's already done. Think of it like working alongside a real colleague.

📌 Day 2 Key Takeaways

  • Cowork can read and analyze your existing files — documents, spreadsheets, PDFs
  • For presentations, include: topic, audience, slide count, structure, and style
  • Complex tasks get automatically broken into steps — watch progress in real time
  • You can redirect Claude mid-task without starting over
  • Put files you want Claude to work with in the folder you've granted access to

Day 3: Power Features & Building Your Workflow

Learn connectors, scheduled tasks, global instructions, and plugins to make Cowork a daily productivity tool.

⏱ ~60–90 minutes

🛠️ Lesson 3.1 — Global & Folder Instructions

Instead of repeating the same context every time, set permanent instructions that Claude follows automatically.

Global Instructions (apply to every session)

1
Go to Settings → Cowork in Claude Desktop
2
Click "Edit" next to Global Instructions
3
Type your instructions (see example below)
4
Click Save
I am a VP of Operations at a mid-size tech company. I prefer concise, professional language. When creating documents, use a clean format with headers and minimal jargon. My preferred file format for reports is Word (.docx). Always show me your plan before making changes to existing files.

Folder Instructions (apply to a specific folder)

Useful when different projects have different needs. For example, in a "Q1 Reports" folder:

This folder contains Q1 2026 financial reports. All documents should use our standard template: company header, date in the header, and page numbers in the footer. Numbers should be in USD format with commas.

Exercise: Go to Settings → Cowork and write 2–3 sentences about who you are, what kind of work you do, and how you prefer things formatted. This small investment improves every future task.

🔌 Lesson 3.2 — Connectors: Linking Your Other Tools

Connectors let Claude pull information from other apps you already use — without you copy-pasting anything.

How to Browse Connectors

1
Go to Settings → Connectors → Browse Connectors
2
You'll see two tabs: Web and Desktop Extensions
3
Enable the ones relevant to your work

Most Useful Connectors for Non-Technical Users

  • Google Drive — Claude can search and read your Docs and Sheets
  • Gmail — Claude can search your email for context
  • Google Calendar — Claude can check your schedule
  • Slack — Claude can read Slack channels for context
  • Notion — Claude can access your Notion workspace

Claude in Chrome (Browser Extension)

This lets Claude interact with websites — filling forms, pulling data from web pages, or researching across multiple sites.

1
Install "Claude in Chrome" from the Chrome Web Store
2
In Claude Desktop, click your initials (bottom left) → enable Chrome Connector
3
When starting a task, enable "Claude in Chrome" in the Connectors dropdown
The Chrome connector only works with Haiku 4.5, Sonnet 4.5, or Opus 4.5 models. Selecting a different model will disable it.

Exercise: Enable Google Drive (or another connector relevant to you), then try:

Search my Google Drive for any documents related to [a project name]. Summarize what you find and list the most relevant files.

⏰ Lesson 3.3 — Scheduled Tasks: Put It on Autopilot

Set up tasks that run automatically — daily, weekly, or on any schedule you choose. This is one of Cowork's most powerful features.

How to Create a Scheduled Task

Method 1: In any Cowork task, type /schedule — Claude walks you through setup.

Method 2: Click "Scheduled" in the left sidebar to view, create, and manage tasks.

Your computer must be awake and the Claude Desktop app must be open for scheduled tasks to run. If your computer is asleep, Cowork will run the task when you next open the app.

Practical Examples

Daily Morning Briefing:

/schedule

Every weekday morning, check my Gmail and Google Calendar. Create a one-page briefing document with: today's meetings and key deadlines, any urgent emails that need attention, and a suggested priority order for my day. Save it to my "Daily Briefings" folder.

Weekly File Cleanup:

/schedule

Every Friday at 4 PM, scan my Downloads folder. Move documents to "Documents," images to "Images," and anything older than 30 days to "Archive." Give me a summary of what was moved.

Weekly Status Report Template:

/schedule

Every Friday, create a Word document called "Week of [date] Status Update." Include sections for: completed items, in-progress work, and next week's priorities. Use placeholder content I can fill in. Save to my "Reports" folder.

Exercise: Type /schedule in Cowork and create a simple recurring task — a daily file organization or a weekly report template.

After the first run, Claude may rewrite your prompt to be more precise based on what it learned. This is normal and usually makes future runs better.

🧩 Lesson 3.4 — Plugins: Pre-Built Superpowers

Plugins are pre-configured packages that make Claude instantly good at specific types of work. Think of them as "skill packs."

What's Inside a Plugin

  • Skills — Domain knowledge Claude draws on automatically
  • Slash commands — Quick actions triggered by typing "/" (like /schedule)
  • Connectors — Links to relevant tools and data sources
  • Sub-agents — Specialized mini-assistants for specific subtasks

🗓️ Lesson 3.5 — Building Your Daily Workflow

Here's a framework for making Cowork part of your routine:

Morning Routine (5 minutes)

  1. Open Claude Desktop
  2. Let your scheduled morning briefing run
  3. Review the briefing and adjust your priorities

During the Day (as needed)

  • "Summarize this 20-page document in one page"
  • "Create a slide deck for my 2 PM meeting"
  • "Draft a professional response to this email" (paste the email in)
  • "Analyze this spreadsheet and highlight the top 10 customers by revenue"

End of Day / Weekly

  • Let scheduled cleanup tasks run
  • Review auto-generated weekly report templates and customize before sending

Prompt Templates to Keep Handy

Quick Document Summary:

Read [filename] and give me a 5-bullet summary of the most important points.

Meeting Prep:

I have a meeting about [topic] in one hour. Create a one-page prep document with: background on the topic, 3 questions I should ask, and key points I should raise.

Email Draft:

Draft a professional email to [recipient] about [topic]. Tone should be [friendly/formal/urgent]. Key points to include: [list them].

Data Analysis:

Open [spreadsheet name]. Tell me: what are the top trends? Are there any outliers or concerns? Create a summary table of the key findings.

📌 Day 3 Key Takeaways

  • Global instructions save you from repeating context every time
  • Connectors link Claude to your tools — Gmail, Drive, Slack, Calendar, etc.
  • Scheduled tasks automate recurring work — daily briefings, weekly reports, file cleanup
  • Plugins give Claude pre-built expertise for specific job functions
  • Build a simple morning / during-the-day / end-of-day routine for maximum value
